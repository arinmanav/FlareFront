    What The Calendar Measures

    Qualifying hotspot detections inside the selected county boundary. Type 0 records qualify at MODIS confidence ≥ 30 or Suomi-NPP nominal/high confidence. Exact repeated location/date/time/satellite keys are removed; separate overpasses remain separate observations.

    Missing And Zero Values

    Unavailable source periods stay missing. Zero means no qualifying detections were reported. Point files do not establish complete observing coverage or the absence of fire.

        Prediction Methods And Limits

        Seasonal History: 2013–2019 VIIRS counts for each grid cell and calendar month, divided by the number of training days in that month, then summed over each future week. Recent VIIRS: counts in the 28-day input interval. This rule has no within-horizon timing model, so ties select the earliest week.

        History + Recent VIIRS: equal weight on seasonal and recent rankings after separately normalizing each over all candidate area-weeks. History + MODIS / VIIRS: the same rule with the recent term replaced by Terra + α × VIIRS. Aqua is retained for calendar comparison but is not used in this rule. These combined values are ranking scores, not incident counts or probabilities; overlapping sensor observations may be counted twice.

        County α uses only 2013–2019. Grid size, equal weights and scoring were fixed for this exploratory comparison. No model is selected after looking at these results. Zero-information ties select the earliest week, then the southernmost and westernmost cell. Equal degree cells and county clipping create unequal areas; this is an unadjusted cell-selection task.

          Count Comparability

          Terra-reference scaling beats the training seasonal median in Los Angeles, San Diego and Santa Clara on the 2020–2025 monthly comparison. Sacramento does not pass. A scaling factor adjusts magnitude; it cannot align overpass times or create missing observations.

          Location And Time

          The combined rule has no consistent advantage across counties. Adding Terra improves this score in Sacramento, leaves San Diego and Santa Clara unchanged, and reduces it in Los Angeles. Recent VIIRS alone has the most matches overall. These results do not establish better ignition forecasting.

          Multimodal Contribution

          The project connects satellite history, forecast timing checks and five quality-filtered spectral indices in one workflow. The available weather and spectral samples do not establish improved prediction. Personnel allocation is a possible downstream use, not an evaluated outcome.

          Alternative Harmonization Results

          Eight alternatives to the fixed ratio were compared using expanding validation folds inside 2013–2019. The selected Los Angeles solar-period model reduced training-period validation error, but its 2020–2025 monthly MAE increased from 4.925 to 5.142. No alternative qualified for the other counties. The original ratio is retained; Sacramento does not meet the acceptance criterion.

          Daily and weekly bins were also checked. They did not resolve Sacramento's weak agreement. The monthly chart connects actual monthly totals without smoothing. These alternative-model results are exploratory because the benchmark years had already been inspected.

          Additional Contribution

          Five Hyperspectral Indices

          NDWI, NDVI, MSI, PSRI and PRI describe different spectral responses in the same EMIT scene. They are correlated features from one sensor, not five independent sensing modalities. Each index retains its own usable-pixel count and actual band centers.

          Scene Quality And Provenance
          NDWI = (R860 − R1240) / (R860 + R1240)

          Gao NDWI. Use reflectance, valid wavelengths and quality masks. Clouded, invalid or unavailable samples cannot contribute an index.

          Required Evidence And Evaluation
          • Verified eligible station locations, reserve constraints and road-travel-time estimates.
          • A forecast issued by the decision time, with valid periods covering the prediction window.
          • Features and model training limited to information available before the cutoff.
          • Independent incident outcomes and matched held-out episodes for weather-only, weather-plus-history and weather-plus-history-plus-spectral comparisons.
          • Uncertainty across episodes and explicit handling of missing imagery. A tie preserves the baseline placement.
